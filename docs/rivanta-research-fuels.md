## Primary external sources and acceptable claims

1. ICAO SAF conversion processes: https://www.icao.int/SAF/saf-conversion-processes
   ASTM-qualified routes distinguish feedstock/technology and blending limits. Biomass can potentially support Fischer-Tropsch routes; biomass-derived alcohols can potentially support alcohol-to-jet. Do not infer that any biomass oil or biogas is immediately aviation fuel. Avoid quoting the page's dated July 2023 total count of approved pathways as a current total.

2. ICAO lifecycle methodology: https://www.icao.int/CORSIA/fuels-lifecycle
   Lifecycle emissions include sourcing/collection, processing, transport, conversion, distribution and aircraft combustion, with land-use effects assessed as applicable. SAF does not mean zero tailpipe CO2. Specific reductions require pathway and lifecycle evidence.

3. ICAO eligible fuels: https://www.icao.int/CORSIA/corsia-eligible-fuels
   CORSIA eligibility requires compliance with sustainability criteria and certification through an ICAO-approved Sustainability Certification Scheme. Technical fuel qualification and sustainability qualification are distinct requirements.

4. Ministry of Civil Aviation/PIB, 29 July 2026: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2291473&lang=2&reg=48
   India was preparing SAF accounting, certification, traceability and policy arrangements. Draft SAF policy was still under preparation. Do not call draft proposals enacted mandates.

5. Government of India Rajya Sabha response, 15 December 2025: https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/269/AU1737_T6tK0c.pdf?source=pqars
   Government-approved indicative SAF blending targets: 1% by 2027, 2% by 2028, 5% by 2030, initially for international flights. Use indicative, not universal aviation mandate. Context is national opportunity, not Rivanta's secured market or guaranteed sales.

6. FICCI primary event press release, 17 November 2026: https://ficci.in/public/press_release_details/5286
   The Civil Aviation Minister said the draft National SAF Policy had been shared with MoPNG for comments. This is the latest primary event record found; it reinforces keeping policy wording conditional rather than treating a final enacted policy as established.

7. Official GOBARdhan/MoPNG CBG explanation: https://gobardhan.gov.in/about/compressed-biogas
   Suitable organic feedstocks undergo anaerobic digestion. Removal of CO2, moisture and impurities enriches the gas before compression. Potential applications include compatible transport and industrial fuel uses. Do not promise benefits without plant-specific operating evidence.

8. GAIL current quality specifications: https://www.gailonline.com/cbgQualitySpecification.html
   Page references IS 16087:2025 for biomethane and separately lists PNGRB pipeline-supply quality. Methane minimum is 95 mole percent for the listed biomethane standard; pipeline specifications are a separate acceptance context. Do not cite the superseded 2016 edition from older CBG documents as current.

9. MNRE biogas programme: https://biogas.mnre.gov.in/about-the-programmes
   Raw biogas contains methane, CO2 and trace gases; upgrading and compression produce CBG. Keep descriptions general rather than applying small-scale programme yield or pressure figures to Rivanta.

10. MNRE R&D review (IIT Delhi paddy straw pretreatment research, historical project): https://research.mnre.gov.in/api/public/R%26D_division_reports/R%26D_Review_Report.pdf
    Technical source supports the need to characterize paddy straw, assess pretreatment, digestion performance and economics. It does not verify Rivanta's technology or imply all residue types behave identically.

11. CPCB Guidelines for Utilisation of Treated Effluent in Irrigation, September 2019: https://cpcb.gov.in/NGT/Guidelines-UTE-Irrigation.pdf
    ZLD means industrial effluent is recycled within processes without discharge. Treatment/concentration/evaporation may be needed; technical and economic feasibility varies. Discharging effluent onto farmland is not equivalent to process-water ZLD. ZLD is not zero air emissions, zero solid waste or proof of a working plant.

## Suggested concise public copy

### SAF
Rivanta is evaluating biomass-based Sustainable Aviation Fuel pathways. Suitable biological intermediates may support alcohol-to-jet conversion, while gasification and synthesis offer another potential route. Development depends on qualified technology, fuel specifications, sustainable sourcing and verified lifecycle performance. This is a proposed workstream, not a claim of current certified fuel production.

### CBG
Suitable organic streams can be processed through anaerobic digestion to produce biogas. Cleaning, methane enrichment and compression can make the gas suitable for qualified transport and industrial applications. Feedstock preparation and digestion design must match the material. Digestate may support nutrient recovery where its quality, processing and intended use meet applicable requirements.

### Water and circularity
Water efficiency and Zero Liquid Discharge are proposed design objectives. The approach would prioritize treatment, process-water reuse and responsible management of concentrated residuals. Feasibility and operating performance require detailed engineering and regulatory review. Energy recovery, renewable utilities and beneficial by-product uses should be evaluated for each suitable stream, without claiming every output is automatically useful or emission-free.
